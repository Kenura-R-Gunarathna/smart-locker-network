Smart Locker Network

Modular lockers where students leave bags and laptops, and borrow lab and sports equipment. Every use is recorded automatically, so staff no longer collect student IDs by hand.

Pilot proposal for university administration.

See how it works What we ask of the university

What students and staff deal with today

Storing things and borrowing things on campus both depend on people and paper.

Nowhere safe to leave things

Most buildings have no place to put a bag or a laptop. Students carry everything with them all day.

Borrowing relies on one person

Lab and sports items are issued by a staff member who takes the student's ID and writes the loan down. If something goes missing, there is no clear record of who had it or when.

No room for a locker room

Many university buildings, especially government ones in Sri Lanka, have no space set aside for fixed lockers.

Lockers built as movable modules

Plywood lockers slide into a rack. Take a locker out, plug it into a rack somewhere else, and it works there.

  1. LockA 12 V solenoid lock that stays locked without power, with a key for staff to override.
  2. Door sensorTells the system whether the door is open or closed.
  3. Status LEDShows from across the room whether the locker is empty, reserved, open or occupied.
  4. BuzzerSounds when a door is left open or something looks wrong.
  5. Beam gridEight light beams sense whether something is inside and roughly how big it is.
  6. Locker nodeA small microcontroller that reads the sensors and drives the lock.
  7. Bus connectorOne 6-pin plug carries the RS485 data pair, ground, power and two address lines to the rack, so a locker can be unplugged and moved.

From reservation to return

Students use a mobile app. The locker LED changes at each step.

  1. Reserved

    Reserve

    The student picks a locker or an item in the app, signed in with their student ID.

  2. Open

    Unlock

    The phone opens the locker over Bluetooth with a time-limited code, so it still works if the network is down.

  3. Occupied

    Use

    The student closes the door. Sensors record the door and whether something is inside.

  4. Empty

    Return

    The student takes or returns the item and closes the door. The locker is free again and the record is saved on the server.

In the web dashboard, staff see what is out, who has it and what is overdue. Each rack also reports its GPS position, so a moved rack shows up on the map.

How the pieces connect

Lockers talk to their rack, racks talk to each other by radio, and one rack sends everything online.

App and dashboard

Mobile app for students, web dashboard for staff.

Phones unlock a locker directly over Bluetooth at the rack. Inside each rack, the lockers share a single cable that carries RS485 data and power. Lockers and racks can be added without changing any code: each locker gets its address automatically, the rack scans its bus and reports what it finds, and the server holds the list of racks and lockers.

The pilot

Two racks, each with 2 or 3 lockers, plus the mobile app, the web dashboard and the online server.

The second rack lets us show location tracking and the link between racks. The pilot runs next to the existing manual register, so students are never blocked if something fails.

What we ask of the university

  • A lab or sports store to pilot in, and a staff member to sponsor it.
  • Wall space and a power outlet for two racks.
  • Agreement on which items are included and how long they can be borrowed.
